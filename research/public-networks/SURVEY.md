## Does a public swarm make sense?

Yes when strangers can contribute independently useful work: sharing public model/dataset files, running complete inference requests, generating/evaluating rollouts, or completing bounded training contributions. The network can persist while individual jobs and worker groups change. A durable discovery/incentive network need not imply an endless training run.

Public model sharding is harder: each output token can depend on several remote workers; WAN latency, churn, inconsistent model versions, and dishonest capacity claims affect the whole request. Privacy and compute-result verification also need explicit mechanisms. [SwarmLLM's README](https://github.com/enapt/SwarmLLM) describes cross-continent slowdowns and the limits of public-swarm privacy.

[IPFS content addressing](https://docs.ipfs.tech/concepts/content-addressing/) lets recipients check received bytes against the requested CID. A hash of an AI worker's answer does not establish correct model execution; that needs recomputation, redundancy, verifiable computation, trusted hardware, or an explicitly weaker quality test. This helps explain why public AI designs often add payment, validators, reputation, or controlled admission.

**Research direction:** Bittensor subnet membership/endpoint authorization and provider markets are stronger evidenced public targets than a hypothetical LocalAI swarm. Audit exact subnet/service boundaries: Chutes has configured validators, Akash has provider/lease isolation, and Cocoon has attestation/registry/proxy boundaries. SwarmLLM is closer to Hivemind/IPFS technically, but currently has much weaker adoption evidence. These are candidate questions, not validated vulnerabilities.
